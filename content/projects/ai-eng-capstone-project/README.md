By submitting your video, you will be asked to sign a short media release confirming you are okay with this. If you prefer your video stays private to your cohort, tell your instructor — it will **not** affect your grade or graduation.

This is not a syllabus walkthrough. Do not narrate weeks or tickets. Pitch the product. A smart hiring manager who is not an engineer should understand the value in one sitting.

### Recording quality checklist

A few minimums so the video looks and sounds professional:

- Horizontal (landscape) orientation — no vertical video
- Good lighting — face a window or lamp; don't sit with a bright light behind you
- Clean audio — a headset or earbud mic is fine; your laptop mic from across the room is not. Record in a quiet space.
- Readable screen recordings — record your demo at full resolution and zoom in on the parts that matter
- Look at the camera during the introduction and the Q&A section at the end (during the demo, it's fine to look at your screen)

### Presentation tips

- Speak naturally — do not read word-for-word
- Practice out loud at least twice before recording
- Focus on the story behind your project
- Explain concepts clearly, even for someone who is not technical
- Show your personality and passion for what you built
- It doesn't need to be perfect — it needs to be you, clearly explaining real work

The goal is not just to show what you created, but to demonstrate that you can communicate the value of an AI solution — the skill that gets you hired.

## 💻 What You Need to Do

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** Field names, entity names, KPIs, and the workflow you demo must match your CONTEXT.md. A generic demo that ignores the company you have been building will not be accepted.

### Recording quality

- [ ] Horizontal (landscape) video — no vertical video
- [ ] Good lighting — face a window or lamp; don't sit with a bright light behind you
- [ ] Clean audio — headset or earbud mic; not a laptop mic from across the room. Quiet space
- [ ] Screen recordings at full resolution, zoomed in on the parts that matter
- [ ] You look at the camera during the introduction and during the entire Q&A section (during the demo, looking at the screen is fine)

### Video structure

Stay near **5 minutes**. Use this structure.

#### 1. 🪝 Introduction — Open with a Hook (30 seconds)

Don't start with your name. Start with the problem or the result, in one sentence — then introduce yourself.

> “Restaurants in Miami lose about 4 hours a week answering the same WhatsApp questions. I'm Ana, and I built an AI agent that answers them automatically.”

Then briefly cover:

- Your name (and background, if relevant)
- Your project name
- The business or process you automated
- A one-line overview of what you built

- [ ] First sentence is the problem or the result — not your name
- [ ] You then cover your name (and background if relevant), the project name, the business or process you automated, and a one-line overview of what you built

#### 2. 🤷 The Problem & Opportunity (45 seconds – 1 minute)

Explain the problem your AI solution addresses:

- What challenge or inefficiency did you identify?
- Who experiences this problem?
- Why is solving it valuable?

- [ ] You name the challenge or inefficiency, who experiences it, and why solving it is valuable

#### 3. 🤖 Your AI Solution (2 minutes)

This is the core of your presentation. Explain:

- What did you build?
- How does it work?
- What AI technologies, tools, or concepts did you use?
- What makes your solution useful?

Include a demonstration:

- Show the application working
- Walk through the workflow
- Explain the user experience

Focus on communicating the value, not only the technical details. Imagine your viewer is a smart hiring manager who is not an engineer.

- [ ] You explain what you built, how it works, which AI technologies / tools / concepts you used, and what makes the solution useful — in language a non-engineer hiring manager can follow
- [ ] You include a demonstration: the application working, the workflow, and the user experience (not only architecture slides)

#### 4. 👷 Engineering Decisions & Challenges (45 seconds)

Every real project involves trade-offs and obstacles. Show how you think like an engineer:

- What was the hardest technical problem you hit — and how did you solve it?
- What trade-off or design decision did you make, and why? (e.g., “I chose X over Y because…”)
- What are you most proud of in how you built this?

Talk like an engineer, not a student. Instead of “I learned how embeddings work,” say “I had to get retrieval accuracy up, so I tested three embedding models and switched to X.” The first sounds like homework; the second sounds like a hiring decision.

- [ ] You name the hardest technical problem you hit and how you solved it
- [ ] You state one trade-off or design decision in the form “I chose X over Y because…”
- [ ] You say what you are most proud of in how you built this — as an engineering decision, not as a course reflection

#### 5. 🗣️ Your 4Geeks Story — Q&A Closing (30–45 seconds)

Close your video by answering the three questions below, speaking directly to the camera. Answer them exactly as written — this section may be edited into Q&A-style videos alongside your classmates' answers, so consistency matters.

Recording rules for this section:

1. Answer in complete sentences that include the question. Your answer must make sense on its own.

- ❌ “Probably time, yeah.”
- ✅ “What almost held me back was my full-time job — I didn't think I'd have the hours.”

1. Pause for **2 full seconds** before and after each answer. This gives editors a clean cut point.
2. Look directly at the lens for this whole section.

The questions (answer all three):

1. What made you decide to pursue AI Engineering — and what almost held you back?
2. Was there a moment, project, or person during the program when things really clicked for you?
3. What would you tell someone who's on the fence about joining 4Geeks?

- [ ] You answer all three questions exactly as written, in complete sentences that include the question
- [ ] You pause 2 full seconds before and after each answer
- [ ] You look directly at the lens for this whole section
