# CONTEXT — HealthCore: Agent Observability (Part 1)

> This document covers Part 1 only. Part 2 (control) reuses the same entities and agent identifiers defined here.
>
> ⚠️ **Non-negotiable constraint:** HealthCore operates under HIPAA (US) and UK GDPR (UK). No patient identifier or Protected Health Information (PHI) may appear in any event, payload, log, or persisted task record generated by your flow — not even as an illustrative example. This applies to every field described below.

## 1. Introduction

The ticket comes from **James Osei**, CTO. HealthCore Digital runs two agents in production with no shared way to check on either one: the **Compliance assistant** that Claire Whitfield's team relies on to answer staff questions about HIPAA and UK GDPR, and the **multi-agent proposal pipeline** that processes institutional RFPs (occupational health contracts, corporate wellness, referral partnerships) for Tom Callahan's Revenue Cycle team. James wants his team to see, at a glance, whether either agent is healthy — without any patient data ever touching this panel.

## 2. Agents You're Observing

| `agent_id` | Name | Type | Owner / Area | Participates in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `compliance_assistant` | Compliance assistant | Single-agent, conversational | Claire Whitfield — Compliance | `chat_session` flows |
| `rfp_pipeline` | Institutional proposal pipeline | Multi-agent (orchestrator, generators, evaluators, synthesizer) | Tom Callahan — Revenue Cycle | `rfp_workflow` flows |

Don't change either agent's internal logic — you're only instrumenting the points where they already act, to emit and persist events.

## 3. Flow Types and `action_type` Taxonomy

| `flow_type` | Description | Typical `action_type` sequence |
|---|---|---|
| `chat_session` | A single conversation between HealthCore staff and `compliance_assistant` | `query` (retrieve relevant policy context) → `tool_call` (if it looks up a policy document) → `deliver` (final answer) |
| `rfp_workflow` | One institutional RFP going through intake, drafting, and evaluation | `query` (classify/extract from PDF) → `tool_call` (fetch department data) → `draft_start` / `write` (department section drafted) → `evaluate` (evaluator pass, including the mandatory PHI check) → `deliver` (synthesizer hands off the consolidated draft) |

Use the `department_id` values already defined for HealthCore's RFP pipeline — `revenue`, `clinical`, `compliance` — as part of the task payload whenever a step belongs to a specific department's generator or evaluator.

## 4. Entity Fields Specific to HealthCore

- **Agent.needs_intervention** must turn `true` automatically when: a `chat_session` has been in `query`/`tool_call` for more than 20 seconds without a `deliver`, an `rfp_workflow` task has looped through `evaluate` more than the configured retry limit, **or** the `compliance` evaluator flags `contains_phi: true` on any section — this last case is always high priority.
- **Flow.triggered_by**: for `chat_session`, the staff member's inbound message (never a patient); for `rfp_workflow`, the RFP ticket being registered (reuse the same `ticket_id` your pipeline already generates).
- The `chat_session` user is always internal HealthCore staff, never a patient — reuse this constraint from Part 2's precedent, it applies here too.

No field in any event may ever contain a patient name, diagnosis, or other patient identifier — review every payload before emitting or persisting it.

## 6. Constraints

- Field, agent, and `action_type` names must match this document exactly — don't invent parallel identifiers.
- Do not surface or duplicate the department-level approval logic from the RFP pipeline here — this panel only observes; approval already lives in that milestone's own flow, and Compliance's PHI check remains mandatory there regardless of what this panel shows.
- Persist every task even if no SSE client is connected when it happens, subject to the PHI constraint above.
